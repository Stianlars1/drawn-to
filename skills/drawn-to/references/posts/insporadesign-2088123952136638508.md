# "Light Work" card with physical wall-washer light

## What we see
A single dark product card ("Light Work", credited to @Designownow_) rendered like a physical object: a matte-black slab with a recessed monolith whose bottom edge hides a linear LED bar. Hovering the panel switches the light on - the bar blooms white, a ceiling spotlight washes the card from above, and the logo casts a shadow upward because the light source sits below it. A skeuomorphic brushed-metal slider toggle bottom-right switches "Easy Mode" with a tooltip label; leaving the card resets everything to dark.

## Layout & composition
- Canvas ~1500×1500 (analyzed at 800×798), background #151515 with radial vignette to #0f0f0f at corners; centered single card.
- Card: ~395×530 (≈49% of canvas width), radius ~36px, surface #1d1d1f with a subtle top-lighter vertical gradient; no visible border, only a ~1px top-edge inner highlight.
- Offset hairline frame: a 1px #2e2e2e rounded-rect drawn ~12px OUTSIDE the card (visible mostly at corners, like crop-marks/brackets); the card visually expands into it on hover - reads as a scale of ~1.01–1.02.
- Corner detail: top-right carries a nested reverse-radius cutout (~55×28px), like a SIM-tray tab, drawn only with 1px value shifts - pure modelling, no color.
- Recessed panel ("monolith"): spans x≈260–540 (inset ~28px from card sides horizontally at slot level), from behind the logo down to ~y520 where it dissolves into shadow; its bottom lip at y≈395 is the light slot.
- Light bar: ~270×6px white strip with chamfered/tapered ends (slight perspective bevel) seated in the lip.
- Text block bottom-left, padding ~32px from card edges; toggle block bottom-right, baseline-aligned with the subtitle; hairline divider (1px #303030) under the subtitle spanning ~55% of card width.
- Toggle: ~100×50px outer metallic pill (radius ~24); tooltip pill sits ~14px above it.

## Card anatomy
- Ratio: top ~72% is pure visual (logo + monolith + light), bottom ~28% is content (title, 2-line description, control). No imagery - the "visual" is modelled lighting on the card surface itself.
- Title bottom-left, control bottom-right: reading order is scene → name → explanation → action.
- Logo badge: ~62px teardrop/pin (one sharp corner, three rounded, r≈50%) in #8b8b8b, negative-space quill/feather with an isometric diamond leaf; centered at ~x400, y282.

## Type & color
- Title "Light Work": geometric sans (Sofia Pro / Poppins character), weight ~500, ~26px at 800px scale, tracking ≈ +1%, #f0f0f0.
- Subtitle: same family, weight ~300, ~15px, line-height ~1.5, Title Case, #9b9b9b.
- Tooltip "Easy Mode Off/On": ~11px, #a0a0a0, letterspaced, in a pill with 1px #2f2f2f border, radius ~14px.
- Palette: bg #151515, card #1d1d1f→#232325, monolith #0d0d0d, light bar core #ffffff with wide bloom, logo #8b8b8b, divider #303030; toggle bezel gradient #e8e8e8→#c9c9c9, inner track #0a0a0a, knob #cfcfcf, lit indicator #ffffff.
- Contrast strategy: near-monochrome; the ONLY saturation-free "accent" is luminance itself - the white light bar carries all hierarchy when active. Off-state contrast is deliberately whisper-quiet (2–4% value steps between planes).

## Graphic language
- Skeuomorphism via light, not texture: no wood/leather; materials are implied purely by shading - matte plastic card, anodized-metal toggle bezel, glass-hot LED core.
- Physically consistent lighting rig: bar below the logo → logo drop shadow projects UPWARD onto the hood; simultaneous overhead spotlight halo above the card top edge; faint diagonal light shafts spill from the bar's ends downward. Three coordinated light sources sold as one event.
- The toggle is a real machined part: outer bevel highlight, recessed track with inset shadow, sliding fader cap, and a paint-white indicator window that "illuminates" in the on state.
- Copy is part of the joke: "Light Work" - the product literally does light work; toggle is "Easy Mode".

## Why saved
Owner note: "kul" (cool) - 'Light work' animation.
Verified. What earns it: (1) the lighting is physically coherent - the logo's shadow flips upward because the source is below it, and a matching ceiling spotlight appears above the card; a generic AI page would glow the bar and stop there. (2) Hierarchy is carried entirely by luminance in a near-monochrome scene - off-state planes differ by 2–4% value, so the on-state feels 10x louder without a single hue. (3) Asymmetric easing: fast ignite (~200ms), slow decay (~350ms), exactly how a real lamp reads. (4) The toggle is fully modelled hardware (bevel, recessed track, sliding cap, lit indicator window) with a tooltip that swaps text in place. (5) Details nobody needed but everyone feels: the offset hairline bracket frame, the reverse-radius corner tab, tapered ends on the LED bar, and the state that politely resets when you leave.

## Extractable rules
- Build dark-mode depth from value steps of 2–4% (#151515 bg, #1d1d1f card, #0d0d0d recess) instead of borders.
- Make hover a light source: white core strip ~6px + 40–80px bloom, plus a secondary overhead halo so the scene has two coherent lights.
- Cast shadows AWAY from your fake light source - if light comes from below, shadows go up.
- Ignite fast, decay slow: ~200ms ease-out on, ~350ms fade off.
- Fire all light-linked layers (bar, wash, shadow, halo, 1.01 card scale) on one cue with zero stagger.
- Keep the accent achromatic: in monochrome scenes, luminance is the accent.
- Model physical controls with 3 layers: beveled bezel gradient (#e8e8e8→#c9c9c9), inset near-black track, sliding cap ~250ms ease-out over ~35% of its track.
- Reveal state with a lit indicator window behind the knob, not a color swap of the whole control.
- Label controls with a hover tooltip pill (1px hairline border, ~11px letterspaced text) that swaps text in place on state change.
- Draw a 1px offset bracket frame ~12px outside the card and let hover scale close the gap.
- Reset all interactive state on container mouse-out so the demo loops clean.
- Put scene top ~70%, title bottom-left, control bottom-right; divide text from control zone with a 1px hairline at ~55% width.
