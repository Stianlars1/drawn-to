## What we see

Three tall still panels from a Glaux studio footer/presentation. The first carries a small blue emblem and serif wordmark, a short studio description and copyright above a hillside town and bridge. The second is almost entirely the landscape with a large quiet sky. The third puts three link columns above another part of the settlement. Blue line and stipple drawing sits on a warm cream ground, with water and reflections along the bottom.

## Layout and hierarchy

The scene grows upward from the lower edge. Different hill contours leave distinct empty areas above the drawing; typography occupies those quiet regions rather than being laid across detailed trees. Towers and cypress shapes establish a vertical rhythm against the horizontal water. The bridge, white architecture and dark foliage provide large-scale legibility, while windows, masonry and fine trees reward close inspection. Three tall panels do not prove a mobile breakpoint or the exact dimensions of a full footer.

## Type, color and material

Serif is the primary voice, including the navigation headings and small copy. The blue ink carries both brand and illustration; the cream paper is shared by sky and unprinted building faces. White/cream gaps inside the blue drawing are structural highlights. Texture is made visible through repeated fine marks, stipple and line density. No numeric grain amplitude, ink hex or original drawing process can be asserted from the JPEGs.

## Why it works

The illustration is a place with connected terrain, buildings and water, not a decorative object dropped into a vacant box. The negative sky gives the navigation room and makes the dense drawing feel intentional. One ink family ties the small emblem to the largest landscape. Light paper and a traditional serif create a different emotional register from the library's technical monochrome examples. The footer has a memorable ending while still performing its basic navigation job.

## Motion and evidence limits

All three assets are stills. There is no evidence of parallax, scroll reveal, water animation, mobile layout or a continuous panorama assembled from these panels. Those would be new design decisions. ## Transfer into a website

Use for an editorial studio, hospitality, craft, place-based brand or a deliberately illustrative product identity. Commission or generate an original scene with a reserved text zone and a crop contract for wide and narrow layouts. Keep navigation as real text outside the picture. Recompose terrain for the target aspect ratio; shrinking the whole tall panel makes both illustration and links unreadable. Limit the drawing to one coherent ink/material world.

## Limitations and transfer risks

The smallest gray copy and fine blue detail may disappear at actual footer size. Check navigation contrast and stroke survival after image optimization. A landscape needs a relationship to the brand; it is not automatically appropriate for every SaaS footer. Do not copy the Glaux mark, settlement, bridge composition or legal text.

## Extractable decisions

Choose paper temperature, ink family, type role, horizon/sky allocation and foreground-to-distance detail ladder together. Let terrain shape the negative space. Preserve two readings: the place from a distance and the workmanship close up. Serif body copy, static imagery and a fully illustrative footer are valid directions, not exceptions requiring conversion to grotesque/mono.
