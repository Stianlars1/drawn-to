## What is visible

A pale technical workbench frames a detailed black and silver camera. The camera rotates, separates into lens/body/internal layers and isolates individual parts. A narrow right inspector names the selection; fine grounding lines, small annotations and an explosion control surround the object.

## Composition, material and visual weight

The lens combines multiple concentric rings, fine grip ribs, transmissive green-tinted elements and an inner aperture. The body has articulated top controls, panel seams, fasteners, circuitry and a different roughness from metal edges. Shadows remain tied to physical parts during the exploded state. Layer count alone is not the quality: the interfaces between parts make the object plausible.

## Timeline and interaction evidence

0-2s changes from assembled to exploded. At about 3-6s the lens rotates toward the viewer and the separation becomes explicit. Around 7-10s the assembly closes. Roughly 12-16s isolates rings and iris leaves; 18-22s returns to close views of the body and top controls. The cursor and changing inspector support an interactive reading.

## Transferable construction

Model a hierarchy of meaningful subassemblies with a shared assembly axis. Use instancing for repeated knurling/fasteners, real bevels for edge light, separate glass and opaque parts, stable object IDs and one continuous explosion scalar. Let selected parts change camera/description without losing orientation. Keep wide and portrait cameras independently composed.

## Limits and risks

The author's caption claims Three.js, 122 component groups and 1,877 modeled pieces. Those counts and the original source were not independently verified. A recorded close-up cannot prove real-time frame rate or the underlying renderer. Treat component count as a caption claim, not a design acceptance metric.
