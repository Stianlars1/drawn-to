## What is visible

A small thin-outlined rectangular sheet floats in a dark isometric coordinate field. Parallel internal lines follow a passing wave, while the outer contour deforms consistently. A tiny wave-height readout sits below the object.

## Composition, material and visual weight

The form is legible because its silhouette, internal rulings and ground projection agree. Minimal linework can show real depth without textured volume. The restrained ground and large surrounding air put attention on the exact continuity of the wave as it enters and leaves the sheet.

## Timeline and interaction evidence

The 12s clip moves between flat and crested states repeatedly. In the 3.5-4.5s detail window the crest crosses the same sheet; its edges and interior lines deform as one surface. The author explicitly describes it as pure SVG without a 3D library.

## Transferable construction

Project a simple parametric surface into SVG coordinates, updating coherent paths from one wave function. Use the same function for outline and interior ruling, with a stable base grid and readable amplitude control. The render can stop once the wave or user response is still.

## Limits and risks

The original implementation was not supplied, so pure SVG is a stated author technique. The source does not establish hover or drag input. Do not introduce a heavy 3D dependency merely because a flat projection looks spatial.
