Partial exemplar. It earns a place for three measured reasons and against three measured defects. For: (1) the hero is type-as-object at 23.5vw / 600 / -0.07em with nothing else in the first screen but one line, one button and a frieze - the corpus had this only in local-production-heroes (Antigravity) and never at this scale on a light ground; (2) the single-asset discipline of F3 applied to photography: three images reused 25 times at 7 crops, the desaturated variants (`grayscale()` on strip 6, `grayscale(.5)` on thumbs, `brightness(.82)` on unselected tiles) doing the hierarchy that a second image set would otherwise do; (3) a warm, green-shifted neutral system (paper #f4f2e9 / ink #1f332c / dark #101310) with every gray hue-locked to the ink - the light cousin of TheKartikBansal-2089's cream-and-warm-black. Against: (a) the capability list is illustrated by a photo that fits every row equally (the swap test fails: "Make something" shows a lake house), so it is a picture beside a list, not the feature shown (C2); (b) entrance choreography on every heading and a letter-by-letter H1 (C12 tell) with 1.3-1.8 s durations, twice the corpus's 400-800 ms; (c) three floating photos at -10/+10/+15 deg in the "common" scene, a named tell, shipped in the source below the fold. It is the best evidence in the corpus of what one-shot generation gets right (scale, restraint, asset reuse, weight 400 headings) and where it defaults (unsystematic radii, decorative imagery, choreography everywhere).

## Limitations and transfer risks
