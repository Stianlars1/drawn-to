## What we see

Four stills show Payper onboarding inside a tablet-like presentation frame. The first introduces sign-in beside a layered app mark. The next three show personal details, username and business details beside a profile preview. The left half owns actions; the slightly lighter right half owns their visible consequence. This is a sequence of related states, not four alternative page designs.

## Layout and hierarchy

The inner viewport divides close to equally into two full-height planes (visual estimate, Tier C). A narrow centered form column leaves substantial side margins. Brand, title, helper text, fields and primary button form one vertical reading path. Progress moves from one to three green segments while the preview stays in the same region. Back is a small outlined control beside the wide primary action. Legal and supporting copy sit below the working area. The bezel belongs to the presentation, not the product UI.

## Type and material

Near-black form ground, slightly lighter preview ground, bright off-white action, restrained green progress. Most elements have almost no chroma. The app mark in photo_1 has a dark top plane, reflective side edge and distinct stacked silhouette; a broad local halo separates it from the background. Photos_2-4 replace that sculptural brand object with a quieter flat profile card. This change in material follows the task stage rather than decorating every field. Type is an understated sans with a large title-to-helper contrast; the smallest helper copy needs a delivered-size legibility check.

## Why it works

The user can see what they are creating while entering information. A stable split, stable preview position and short form make each step feel manageable. The white action is the strongest local contrast; the green progress line provides orientation without competing with it. The welcome object establishes brand presence once, then steps back so the user-generated profile becomes the subject. Repeating the card grammar is appropriate here: continuity is the information.

## Motion and evidence limits

These are four still images. Live mirroring, validation timing, transitions, focus behavior and mobile stacking are NOT demonstrated. The apparent relationship between entered names and preview text supports a preview-as-consequence pattern; it does not prove an implemented live binding. Do not invent a spring, duration or shader from the stills.

## Transfer into a website

Use for onboarding, a profile builder, configuration or a signup flow where a preview answers an actual user question. Bind form and preview to the same state in a new implementation; show honest empty states and validation. Keep the preview secondary on small screens and ensure the next action remains reachable with the keyboard open. Reuse the result-card silhouette across steps. For a marketing page, demonstrate this real flow as one feature rather than attaching a generic form to an unrelated glossy icon.

## Limitations and transfer risks

Very dim helper text and input boundaries may lose readability; measure contrast in the implementation. Small progress segments require a textual current-step label. The supplied designs do not establish accessibility, responsive behavior or successful submission. Do not transfer the third-party logo, trust badges, copy or implied social integrations.

## Extractable decisions

Lock the relationship first: action on one side, visible consequence on the other. Separate task planes with tone, not two floating cards. Put the loudest material on the introduction and quieter material on ongoing work. Preserve spatial continuity across stages. Treat this as a sequence: apply the ORDER test, not a requirement for a different device on each step.
