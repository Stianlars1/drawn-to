# Three claims, three isometric worlds, one register

## What we see
A three-card feature section for x402 (an HTTP-native payment protocol), rendered as isometric line drawings on white. One wide card on top and two half-width cards below, each carrying its own animated isometric scene and a bottom-left text block (eyebrow / headline / two-to-three line body). Nothing is a screenshot, an icon or a stock 3D object: each card is a small world built from the same unit cube, and each world argues its own card's claim. The loop is 5.017 s at 60 fps (301 frames, exported 2592x2160 = 2x of 1296x1080 CSS).

The three claims and their scenes:
1. **THE PROBLEM - "Payments on the internet are fundamentally flawed."** A walled maze at building scale fills the card. A thin route line threads it; black extruded label plates reading "Card Number…", "Expiration Date…", "CVV…", "Country…" surface along the route, queue up, and dissolve without ever leaving the maze. The form is drawn as terrain you have to walk.
2. **AGENT-READY - "Made for machines, not just humans."** Two machine bodies float over the grid: a box whose black face carries a white aperture/target glyph (the client), and a tilted open carton whose black panel carries a white mark (the counterpart). A dash train travels the axis between them. Nothing else is on stage - no form plate, no cursor, no human proxy. The empty space between the two bodies is the argument.
3. **HTTP-NATIVE - "x402 is built into the internet."** A dense cluster of cubes and towers on a pale ground plate - a city block, the existing infrastructure. It parts into four groups that slide outward along the iso axes, revealing a recessed dark pit with the protocol's white zigzag mark already sitting on the floor of it. It holds open long enough to read, then closes.

## Layout & composition
- Section content width 1036 CSS px (2073 native), centred on white; no section-level background, no header band in frame.
- Row 1: one card 1036 x 555 (1.87:1). Row 2: two cards 511 x 397 (1.29:1). Gutter 14.5 px both axes (29 native). The wide card's height is ~1.4x the small ones - the biggest claim gets the biggest stage.
- Cards: white fill, 1px border at ~#DCDCDC (measured 220-225 on 255), radius ~0-4, no shadow, no tint. The border is the only separation (C3, hairline rung).
- Every card is filled edge to edge with a hairline isometric diamond grid, clipped by the card. Horizontal repeat ~45 CSS px; line value ~#DBDBDB (measured 219 median, 200 min), ~1 px perpendicular. The grid is a ground plane, not a texture: objects stand on it, shadows fall across it, and the text block sits over it without a scrim.
- Text block: bottom-left of every card at a constant ~44 px inset from the card's left edge; in the wide card it occupies the lower-left quadrant while the maze fills the upper-right, so the illustration and the copy share the card without overlapping glyphs.
- Illustration bleed: the maze runs past the card's right and top edges and is clipped - the world is bigger than the frame. The two small cards keep their objects fully inside, centred in the upper two-thirds.

## Card anatomy
- Order in all three: eyebrow -> headline -> body, left aligned, no CTA, no chip row, no icon.
- Eyebrow: ~12 px, all caps, letter-spacing ~0.12em, #7F7F7F (measured 127), cap height 9 px CSS. One per card - it is the only mono-ish label in the composition.
- Headline: 28-30 px, weight 500-600, line-height 29 px (measured baseline delta), pure black, max 2 lines. Identical size on the wide card and the small cards - the hierarchy comes from stage size, not type size.
- Body: 15 px / 19.5 px leading, near-black (#111-class), measure 52-60 ch, 2-3 lines.
- Vertical rhythm inside the text block: eyebrow -> headline ~36 px, headline -> body ~18 px.
- No card has more than one illustration, and no illustration has more than one black focal element.

## Type & color
- Zero chroma. The entire section is white, four grays and black.
- Value ladder, measured: card border #DCDCDC · grid line #DBDBDB · ground plate/contact shadow #F5F5F5-#EDEDED (measured 247 / 231) · object faces #FFFFFF (top-lit) and #CDCDCD-#CFCFCF (measured 205/207 on the shaded faces) · contour black #0B0C0B · one pure-black face per card carrying a white glyph.
- Text grays: eyebrow #7F7F7F, headline #000, body near-black.
- Type is a single grotesque (Inter/Aeonik-class) across eyebrow, headline and body; the label plates inside the illustrations are set in the same family, italic, sheared onto the iso plane so they read as objects in the world rather than UI on top of it.

## Graphic language
- **Projection**: one isometric camera for all three cards, never moved. Objects are built from a single unit cube whose footprint equals one grid diamond; walls are runs of it, towers are stacks of 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3 units, plazas are fields of it.
- **Contour hierarchy - the thing that makes it read as a drawing rather than clip art**: a heavy uniform black silhouette around every solid (roughly 2-2.5x the internal weight), thinner internal module seams, and the grid thinner still. Three weights, no more.
- **Flat faces**: no gradient on any object face. Depth comes from the value ladder (white top face, gray sides) plus complete occlusion - near solids cover far ones outright; nothing is faded to fake distance.
- **Long runs are one silhouette**: the maze walls are drawn as extruded polylines with the module seams drawn inside them, not as N cubes side by side (side-by-side cubes double the contour and kill the silhouette).
- **Shadows**: projected footprint silhouettes sheared onto the grid at ~#EDEDED with a soft edge. Grounded objects touch their shadow; the two floating machines in card 2 have the same shadow *detached and offset*, and the gap is the altitude. No drop shadow on any object itself.
- **The one gradient in the whole section** is the interior of the opened pit in card 3: near-black at the deepest corner ramping to ~#B8B8B8 at the lip. A cavity is the only thing here allowed to be lit.
- **Diegetic payloads (C10)**: the label plates carry the actual field names of a card form, not "Label 1". The revealed mark is the product's own glyph, flat on the pit floor, readable at 40 px.

## Motion timeline
All three cards run on ONE clock (measured by per-card frame differencing): enter, hold, release, quiet - a 5 s loop that returns to its own first frame.

**Card 3 (the cleanest measurement, by bounding box + ink area per frame):**
- f1-36 (0-600 ms): closed, still. Ink area 31.6 k px.
- f37-112 (617-1867 ms): opens. Four module groups translate outward along their own iso axes; the assembly's top edge rises 92 native px (46 CSS). Ink area 31.6 k -> 60 k as the interior faces are exposed. Position-vs-time is fast-then-settling (ease-out; most of the travel is done by f97).
- f113-186 (1883-3100 ms): holds fully open, pixel-identical for 74 frames (~1.23 s). This hold is what makes the reveal readable.
- f187-264 (3117-4400 ms): closes on the same curve, slightly slower.
- f265-301 (4417-5017 ms): closed, quiet (~600 ms) before the loop repeats.

**Card 1:** the route line is drawn on through the maze (a stroke-draw that starts around f46 and advances along the walls); the label plates surface one after another - "Card Number…" ~f100, "Expiration Date…" ~f139, "CVV…" ~f163, "Country…" ~f175 (stagger ~250-400 ms). They hold as a queue from ~f180-f240, then fade out in the order they arrived while the route's tail sweeps right and exits (~f241-f286). Nothing ever reaches an endpoint: the loop restarts with an empty maze. Measured by connected-component tracking of the black ink in the card.

**Card 2:** the two bodies drift/tilt slowly (ambient, linear) while a dash train of 3-5 dashes (~30 CSS px long, big gaps) travels the iso axis between them at constant velocity; the exchange completes and repeats. Change-vs-first-frame rises from f29 to ~f125, plateaus to ~f180, decays to ~f277.

Registers are cleanly split (C6): the travelling dashes and the drift are linear/ambient; the opening, the plate reveals and the dissolves are eased. Loop closure is near-perfect (residual difference at f301 is under 1% of the peak).

## Why saved
Because the three illustrations are not interchangeable. Each one is derived from its own card's claim - an obstacle you cannot get through, two actors with nothing between them, a host that opens to show the thing was already inside - and mounting any of them on a sibling card would argue something false. That is the standard the skill's illustration engine has to hit: not "a nice isometric", but a scene that could only belong to this feature. Second reason: the craft budget is tiny. Four grays, three line weights, no color, no gradient except one cavity, no texture - and it still reads as expensive, because the contour hierarchy, the occlusion order and the held beat are all correct.

## Extractable rules
1. **Derive the scene from the claim, then test it.** Claim shape picks the topology: friction -> gauntlet, disintermediation -> two actors with empty space, integration -> open the host. Then run the swap test: mount the concept on the sibling features and write the wrong claim it would make. If it survives, it is decoration.
2. **What the loop asserts is part of the concept.** Never-arrives = friction. Opens-and-closes = it was already inside. Passes-directly = no intermediary. A static version of each of these three would argue less.
3. **Hold the reveal.** ~1.2 s of stillness at full open; a reveal without a hold cannot be read.
4. **One register for the set.** One projection, one unit cube, one value ladder, one contour hierarchy, one focal rule (exactly one black face per card), one clock. Variety comes from what is on stage, never from restyling.
5. **Three line weights**: silhouette >> seam > grid. A single-weight iso drawing collapses at a glance.
6. **Occlusion, not opacity**, for depth; flat faces, not gradients, for material. Reserve the only gradient for an interior.
7. **Shadows are projected footprints**, and detaching one is how you say "floating".
8. **Labels are objects**: extruded plates with the text sheared onto the plane, carrying real product strings.
9. **Let the world exceed the frame** on the biggest card (clip the maze at the card edge) - it implies scale the card cannot contain.
10. **Size the stage, not the type**: the same headline size on a card 1.4x taller reads as the primary claim because its world is bigger.
