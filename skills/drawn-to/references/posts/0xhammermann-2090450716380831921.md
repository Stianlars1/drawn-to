# Three claims, three isometric worlds, one construction system

Measured frame by frame across all 301 frames (60 fps, 5.017 s, 2592x2160 export = 2x of 1296x1080 CSS). Every number below is a pixel measurement; the few inferences are marked. Values in CSS px unless stated.

## What we see
A three-card feature section for x402 (an HTTP-native payment protocol): one wide card on top, two half-width cards below, each carrying its own animated isometric scene drawn on a hairline diamond grid. No screenshots, no icons, no 3D-render aesthetic - three small worlds built from ONE unit cube, one projection and one four-step gray ladder.

1. **THE PROBLEM - "Payments on the internet are fundamentally flawed."** A labyrinth of unit cubes on a 21x16 lattice fills the right of the card. A pale spline threads its corridors; a black dash runs that spline like a cursor tracing the maze, at a dead-constant speed, and four black plaques - "Card Number…", "Expiration Date…", "CVV…", "Country…" - drop onto the route as it passes, hold, and lift off together. The tracer exits the card's right edge without arriving anywhere. The maze has no goal marker and no exit.
2. **AGENT-READY - "Made for machines, not just humans."** The same wedge body drawn twice: one square on the grid wearing a lens (ring + centre dot + two ticks) on its single black face, one knocked off-axis in flight with a franked badge on its black end cap. They slide down-right along one iso axis in PARALLEL - identical distance, identical curve - the payload starting 550 ms after the machine. No handshake, no meeting point, no approval beat, no path, no human.
3. **HTTP-NATIVE - "x402 is built into the internet."** A 4x3 plaza of 12 blocks. It parts - every displacement a pure ground-plane slide with a mathematically zero vertical component - to uncover a recess already cut into the floor, with the protocol's exchange mark lying flat in it. Twelve modules in, twelve out; nothing is added, nothing is stacked, nothing arrives from off-screen. The ground itself opens, like a service trench in an existing street.

## Layout & composition
- Section content width ~1037.5. Row 1: one card 1037.5 x 555.5. Row 2: two cards ~511 x 397. Gutter 14.5 both axes. Cards: white, square corners, 1 CSS px border at ~#D2D2D2, no shadow, `overflow: hidden`.
- Ground grid inside every card: two line families at exactly +/-30 deg, horizontal period 53.5 per family, rhombus side 30.9. Ink is roughly 0.15-0.3 CSS px of full-black equivalent (darkest pixels 203-215), i.e. a true hairline. Drawn first, always occluded by the subject, and clipped by the card.
- **The grid is faded to zero behind the copy** - a second, independent mechanism on top of the silhouette clearance. On cards 2 and 3 the fade is vertical: 100% down to ~43% of card height, 70% at 212, 50% at 240, 10% at 305, literally 0 from ~332 (80-82% of the card). On card 1 the fade is diagonal, clearing the lower-left wedge. Two mechanisms doing one job: geometry alone would leave the grid texturing the words; a scrim alone would leave the subject crowding them.
- Subject bleeds off at least one card edge (card 1's maze is clipped by the right edge; its top apex is clean, ~11.5 clear). Neither end of card 1's path is capped - both are cut by the card's clip.
- Copy block: bottom-left, 44 left margin, on cleared white. Minimum clearance from card 1's maze silhouette to the nearest headline glyph: 103.

## Card anatomy
- Order in all three: eyebrow -> headline -> body. No CTA, no chip row, no icon, no legend.
- Eyebrow ~12.6, uppercase, tracked (set widths: THE PROBLEM 107.0, AGENT-READY 112.0, HTTP-NATIVE 105.0), #888888, cap height 8.5, stem 1.46 (0.114em).
- Headline 28-30 / ~29 leading, black, stem 2.66 (0.105em ~ weight 500), max 2 lines, balanced wrap (card 1's lines measure 281 and 288).
- Body 15 / 19.5, near-black, stem 1.32, 2-3 lines.
- Same headline size on the wide card and the small cards: hierarchy comes from stage size, not type size.

## Type & color
- Zero chroma anywhere. Four-step ladder and nothing else: **#FFFFFF faces · #E8E8E8 (232) · #CECECE (206) · #0B0B0B**, plus the card border #D2D2D2 and the grid hairline.
- Cards spend the ladder differently: card 1 uses a single tint (tops #E7E8E7, BOTH side faces pure white); card 2 is binary (every face pure 255, zero variance across 12x12 patches, one black face per object); card 3 tints per MODULE, not per orientation (left faces always white; top and right share one gray - 9 modules at #CECECE, 3 at #E8E8E8, scattered).
- **There is no top/left/right value ramp.** The conventional iso lighting contract (top lightest, left mid, right darkest) is deliberately refused: refusing the third value flattens each block just enough that the cluster reads as a plan rather than a render.
- Exactly one element family per card goes #0B0B0B, and it is the noun the headline is about: card 1 the plaques and the tracer (the form, and the walking); card 2 one face per body (the sensor and the frank); card 3 the strokes of the blocks and the pit seam. Roughly 5-8% of card ink. Two blacks would mean two claims.

## Graphic language
- **Projection: TRUE isometric, 30.00 deg (slope 0.5774)** - measured three independent ways (Radon peak at +/-30.00, grid shear dx/dy 1.730, plaque top edges -0.5773/-0.5772/-0.5767/-0.5769). 2:1 game-iso (26.57 deg) is ruled out by 3.4 deg. The camera never moves, in any card, at any point.
- **One module for the whole section**: a true cube whose projected footprint diamond is ~53.1 x 30.6 with a ~30.9 vertical edge (footprint edge = height). The ground grid cell is the same diamond within 1-3%, so every solid stands on exactly one grid cell.
- **One stroke weight per card, identical on silhouette and interior seam.** Card 1: 2.69-2.75 silhouette vs 5.38-5.49 native seam - statistically identical. Card 2: 2.59 on every edge. Card 3: 2.6 on every edge. The heavier-outer-contour instinct is refused everywhere: with one weight the object reads as a technical assembly rather than a sticker.
- **Stroke COLOR assigns the role instead**: gray-stroked geometry is architecture that must recede (card 1's maze at #B7B8B8, so the black tracer and plaques own the eye); black-stroked geometry is the subject itself (cards 2 and 3). This is the register's substitute for a weight hierarchy.
- **Depth = occlusion + one tint.** No atmospheric fade, no scale falloff, no perspective convergence, no parallax, no ambient occlusion, no bevels, no rounded cube corners, no face gradients. A near solid's opaque white fill simply covers the far solid's contour.
- **Shadows**: card 2 uses a hard, flat isometric polygon at #E8E8E8 with literally zero blur (edge profile 255,253,242,234,232 - a 2-device-px antialias ramp), every edge on +/-30 deg or vertical, rigidly parented (offset constant to +/-0.35, area constant to 0.4% across 301 frames - it never scales, softens or fades). Card 3 uses one soft plate (~281 x 162, #F6F6F6 to #F3F3F3, 8-10 px edge) for the whole composition. **Card 1 has no shadow at all.**
- **The only gradient in the section** is the recess floor in card 3: #0D-#28 along the back edges ramping to #C8 at the front vertex, plus an inner shadow pooled in the back corner (18-25 under the apex vs 84 at the far end of the same edge) - a pure linear ramp would read as a printed gradient.
- **Asymmetric edge treatment on the pit**: a 2.6 px black seam on the two BACK edges, no stroke at all on the two FRONT edges, where the floor's lightest value meets the ground. The front lip dissolves - the hole opens toward you.
- **Plaques are objects in the projection, not UI**: `skewY(-30deg)` on a rounded rect, so long edges run the iso axis and short edges stay screen-vertical. Fill #F2F2F2, outline #0B0B0B 2.65, radius ~7.1 - **the same radius token as the path's corner fillet**. The extrusion is a hard duplicate of the same shape filled #0B0B0B, offset (-9.85, -5.85) along the iso axis and hulled to the plate; projected depth 11.5. All four plaques are the same height (46.2) and differ only in width (129 / 141 / 62.5 / 90), sized to the label.
- **The type inside the plaques is upright roman, not italic**: it looks slanted only because it sits inside the same `skewY(-30deg)` group. The stems come out exactly screen-vertical - which is what the skew does to upright text. Every label ends in a literal ellipsis, so it reads as a placeholder field.
- **The glyph in card 3** is flat on the ground plane with zero extrusion, filled #F3F3F3 (not white) against a #14 floor - the highest-contrast edge in the card. Two chevron bars pointing in OPPOSITE directions, 180-deg point-symmetric: an exchange mark, not an arrow. Extruding it would have made it an object sitting in the hole; keeping it flat makes it painted into the floor.
- **Annotation layers are exempt from depth sorting**: card 1's plaques draw above every cube regardless of position, because they are labels, not bodies.

## Motion timeline
All three cards share one 5 s clock. Registers are strictly separated and each easing carries a fixed meaning: **linear = an indifferent process · ease-out (~1-(1-t)^1.5, ~580 ms) = something arriving and settling · ease-in-out = a body moving under its own power.**

**Card 1 - the never-arriving form.** 167 ms of dead hold at the loop seam with zero black on the card. The tracer's first pixel appears at f36; it runs the 1827-px spline at a dead-constant 497 CSS px/s, never easing, never slowing at a corner, never pausing under a plaque, and exits the card's right edge at f287. Plaques drop in with ease-out (p~1.47, onset f79, 600 ms, translateY ~-21 to -26, bounding box constant to +/-2 px - no scale, no spring) at irregular gaps of ~300 / 800 / 200 ms: the maze dictates when each field appears. They exit together at exactly 100 / 100 / 100 ms stagger - one decision - with a sine ease-in-out on opacity but a dead-LINEAR translate (1.16 native px/frame). Nothing completes: no check, no count-up, no green.

**Card 2 - one idea repeated with a delay.** Agent starts at f21.5 (358 ms), payload at f53.6 (893 ms) - a 550 ms follow lag. Both travel identical distances (29.6 vs 29.3) down-right along the iso axis at 29-30 deg, identical durations (out ~1620 ms, hold ~650 ms, back ~1610 ms), identical easeInOutSine, re-pinning within 0.05 px of frame 1 - a palindrome. Four motion streaks (two per body, born f34 / f60.8 / f64.4 / f97.8, gone by f241) are stroked 2.84 - deliberately HEAVIER than the 2.59 object outline, with round caps - so they read as signal, not structure; they pass BEHIND the bodies (verified: a streak's origin stays white until the body slides clear at f118). No bob, no float, no tilt wobble; 1133 ms of the loop has nothing moving at all.
**Card 3 - the ground opens.** Closed hold ~950 ms spanning the loop seam (last close motion f271, first open motion f28). Opening f28-f119: 12 different start frames converging on ONE shared landing frame (f119 +/- 1.5) - fanning out feels unlocked, landing together feels resolved. Every displacement is a pure ground-plane slide with zero vertical component (measured +125 / -67 native on the two axes). The reveal completes ~450 ms BEFORE the motion does (glyph fully exposed at f92, blocks stop at f119): you read the message, then watch the blocks settle. Open hold ~1267 ms. The close inverts the stagger - one shared start (f183-184), fanning arrivals - and takes ~1467 ms.

Loop closure: cards 2 and 3 are palindromes that re-pin to frame 1; card 1 is a one-way traverse that enters and leaves by different card edges and never returns to a start.

## Why saved
Because the three illustrations are not interchangeable, and that is provable: card 3's opening plaza on card 2 would argue "agent support is a hidden capability inside something existing" (not the claim); card 2's two clean bodies on card 1 would argue "this already works smoothly, unattended" - the exact opposite of "fundamentally flawed"; card 1's maze on card 3 would argue "here is a whole new system to navigate", which is what the copy explicitly denies. Second reason: the craft budget is tiny - four grays, one stroke weight per card, no color, no gradient except one pit, no texture, no idle motion - and it still reads as expensive, because the projection, the occlusion order, the stroke discipline and the held beats are all exactly right.

## Extractable rules
1. **Draw the noun the copy blames or promises - never the product.** A payments section here contains zero images of money: card 1 draws a checkout form, card 2 draws a machine and a franked parcel, card 3 draws a street. Test: you should be able to name the drawn object without saying the product's name.
2. **Claim shape picks the topology.** Cost-shaped claim -> a measurable route through built obstruction, both ends clipped, no start or end marker. Actor-shaped claim -> two posed bodies, one glyph each, zero path (a path would concede the actor has to traverse something). Already-there claim -> a closed solid that OPENS; author the interior first and occlude it 100% at rest, then reveal by subtraction. Never reveal by addition - anything that enters has been argued to be new.
3. **One black per card, and the black IS the claim.** Everything else stays in the gray architecture, so nothing has to compete.
4. **One stroke weight per card; let stroke COLOR carry the layer.** Gray = scenery, black = subject. Never thicken the silhouette.
5. **Refuse the third face value.** Top-lightest / left-mid / right-darkest turns a drawing into a render; two values (or one tint) keep it a plan.
6. **Motion asserts the verb; a still frame can only assert the noun.** Ask what the still already says, then make the motion say the part it cannot.
7. **Tense rule**: a permanent property loops as a palindrome that re-pins to the first frame; a process the user suffers is a one-way traverse that enters off one edge and leaves by another.
8. **Stagger grammar**: irregular gaps read as the scene dictating the pace; exactly regular gaps read as one decision being obeyed. Both are hand-timed.
9. **Land the payoff early.** Finish the reveal ~450 ms before the movement stops; a payoff on the last frame feels like a machine finishing.
10. **Rigid transforms only**: translate along an iso axis, translate on screen-Y, opacity, stroke-width, gradient stops. Never scale, rotate, skew or bob an iso solid, and never let its shadow move with it.
11. **Author the hole where the words go** with two independent mechanisms: the subject's own silhouette vacating a wedge along a projection axis, AND a scrim taking the grid to literally zero ink behind the type.
12. **No explanatory chrome**: no arrowheads, no connectors between the two bodies, no numbered steps, no legend, no success state, no idle motion. Stillness is allowed to be the loop's largest phase.
