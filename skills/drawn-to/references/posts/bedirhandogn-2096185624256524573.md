## Graphic language
- The device: a flat UI treated as an unlit emissive plane in a black void. No material, no bevel, no extrusion, no specular, no vignette, no cast shadow, no ground plane, no reflection - the 3D contributes exactly two things, perspective and depth of field. This is the opposite pole of nilseller-2093, which converts a UI into hardware (extruded chips, brushed surfaces, highlights, LED bars) and belongs to F7; here the UI stays a page, and only the camera is physical.
- The UI's own vocabulary survives the staging intact: bracket corners on the buttons and the logo tile, 1 px hairlines at +2-6% white for the panel and the tab underline, square chips with pin ticks, a schematic wire diagram, mono install copy (F4 vocabulary, documented in the sibling). The blurred ghost row under each chrome element (button, nav row, CTAs), a UI-layer device from the sibling, is still visible in photo_1 under "Get Started" (rows 128-156, +6 luminance).
- The camera is the only ornament. Each shot chooses one thing to be sharp and lets everything else become tone: the claim (photo_1), the install (photo_2), the whole page with the diagram as foreground bokeh (photo_3). No shot has two focal planes.
- Because the ground is the same #070707 inside and outside the panel, the perspective is carried by hairlines and text alone - the eye reads a plane it cannot see.
- Grain and banding: not measurable from these JPEGs (the measuring protocol forbids the audit).

## Why saved
Saved without comment.

It earns its place for a reason the corpus lacked: it is the cleanest demonstration that a hero can be presented as a 3D scene without changing a single UI value. hero-atmosphere.md already asks for genuine depth of field ("one layer sharp, one layer blurred, SAME content") and render-tiers.md places real optics at T6; neither shows what the rule looks like applied to a finished page. This reference measures it: sharp edges 2-3 px, soft edges 13-47 px on a 3840 frame (0.3-1.2% of frame width), exactly one focal plane per frame, 65/30/31% of content sharp, zero bloom, zero lighting, a ground that is identical inside and outside the panel. It also shows the presentation rule that matters most: the focal plane IS the claim. Photo_1 focuses the H1, photo_2 focuses the install line, photo_3 focuses the page and blurs the illustration - three shots, three arguments, one asset.

Positive exemplar for presentation. Partial as a page: the UI underneath ships a tell (below).

## Extractable rules
- Keep the UI plane unlit and unfilled: the ground under the panel must equal the void (#070707 here, both sides), so the plane is defined by hairlines and type only. No material, no specular, no vignette, no cast shadow, no reflection.
- Stage the plane with a camera, not a transform stack: pitch (photo_1: lines horizontal, verticals converge 1.3 deg, upper line 86% the height of the lower), or roll + yaw (photo_2: 3-4 deg roll, 8% recession across the headline; photo_3: 17-25 deg, 36% recession). One rotation family per shot.
- One focal plane per shot, and it must be the claim: the headline band (photo_1), the install line (photo_2), or the whole page with the illustration as foreground blur (photo_3). Never two sharp planes.
- Blur budget, expressed as 10-90% edge width in fractions of frame width: in focus 0.05-0.08%, leaving focus 0.15-0.25%, soft 0.35-1.2%. At a 1440 CSS px hero that converts to ~1 px sharp, 2-4 px leaving, 5-17 px soft (Tier C conversion from ratios).
- Let the blur grow monotonically along the receding axis: photo_2 runs 3 -> 8 -> 11 -> 33 -> 47 px along one tab row. Blur that does not follow depth reads as a filter.
- Let the softened headline become the field: at 4% frame coverage and 15-25 px edge width the amber H1 in photo_2 is the only color mass in the frame. Do not add a separate atmosphere asset behind a staged UI.
- No bloom on the accent: chroma outside a sharp glyph must fall to ground within 2 px. The glow comes from defocus alone; a painted halo on top of it double-counts the light.
- Grade the whole scene, not the element: the staged amber sits at hue 37 deg, chroma 174-199 against the flat view's chroma 156 - same hue, +20-40 chroma, applied globally.
- Keep every UI value intact across shots: the same H1, tabs, install line, brackets and hairlines appear in all three; only the camera moves. The audience must be able to reconstruct the flat page from the renders.
- Magnify the headline 1.5-2.5x relative to its flat size and let it cross one or two frame edges (photo_2 right and bottom, photo_3 bottom); the crop is what says "scene" rather than "screenshot".
- Keep the luminance budget: 85-87% of the frame under 5% luminance, under 2.1% above 50%, one accent hue at 0.7-4% coverage. Nothing reaches #ffffff; the accent is the brightest pixel in the frame.
- Frame the two halves as a constant: diagram left, copy right, copy column edge at 40-60% of frame width; move the camera around that arrangement instead of re-laying out the page per shot.

## Limitations and transfer risks
- Four calls to action in one view: "Get Started" in the nav, the `npm install` line, "Get Started" again as the hero button, and "Documentation". quality-bar's budget is one primary and one secondary; "Get Started" appearing twice on the same screen is the clearest form of the tell.
- The eyebrow "BUILT ON OBJECT STORAGE" above the H1 is the one eyebrow a hero may carry; it is allowed here only because the corpus cannot see the sections below.

## Not measurable from these frames
- Any CSS px value (type sizes, gaps, radii, hairline widths): the capture is a perspective render; see bedirhandogn-2096193 for the flat measurements.
- Grain, banding, dither: JPEG.
- Whether the renders came from a real 3D scene (a plane in a DCC with a thin-lens camera) or a 2D perspective transform with masked blur: both produce these numbers; the monotonic blur along one axis and the converging hairlines are consistent with either.
- The camera's focal length and f-stop: the blur ramp (3 -> 47 px over ~8% of recession) says the aperture is a CG choice, not a photographic one, and cannot be solved from a still.
