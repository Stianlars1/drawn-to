# Toggle switch that stretches on press

## What we see
A 9.15s prototype demo (1600x1200, 30fps) of a single oversized iOS-style toggle switch on a near-white canvas, with a "Plain / Stretch" segmented control beneath it acting as a mode compare. The first half demonstrates the plain toggle (translate + color crossfade only); the presenter then flips the segment to "Stretch" and re-runs the same clicks, now with a knob that elongates toward its destination on mousedown, holds the deformation while pressed, and springs into place on release. The clip is a perfect loop: it ends by switching back to "Plain" in the idle OFF state of frame 1.

## Layout & composition
- Canvas: 1600x1200 (4:3), background #FBFBFB, zero chrome - exactly two elements.
- Switch track: ~800x356px, centered horizontally, vertical center at ~45% of canvas height; full-pill radius (178px). Soft diffuse drop shadow under the track (~0 18px 36px rgba(0,0,0,0.10)) plus a subtle darker inner bottom lip that makes the track read as a recessed well.
- Knob: rounded capsule ~480x304px (60% of track width - deliberately wide, not a circle), inset ~20px from track edges, radius ~150px. Own shadow ~0 8px 16px rgba(0,0,0,0.16) and a hairline rim ~1px rgba(0,0,0,0.06), so it reads extruded above the recessed track. Knob travel distance ~290px.
- Segmented control: ~728x144px container at ~89% canvas height, centered; pill radius, ~16px inner padding, container #F1F1F2 with a faint outer shadow; white sliding thumb ~330x112px with soft shadow (~0 4px 10px rgba(0,0,0,0.08)).
- Vertical gap switch → segmented control: ~290px. Generous negative space everywhere; the component is the whole composition.

## Type & color
- Labels "Plain" / "Stretch": SF Pro / Inter-like grotesque, ~48px at native scale, weight ~500; active label near-black #17181A, inactive #8E9196. No other text on screen.
- Track ON: vivid blue ~#2E6FE9. Track OFF: neutral gray ~#C7C7C7. Mid-crossfade passes through a blue-tinted slate ~#AEBBCB - color interpolates on the same clock as knob travel, never snaps.
- Knob: pure #FFFFFF at all times; state is carried entirely by the track color.
- Contrast strategy: one saturated accent against an otherwise achromatic gray/white scene; depth via layered soft shadows instead of borders.

## Graphic language
No illustration - the component is rendered as a physical object. Two-layer depth model: recessed track (inner lip + large soft ground shadow) with an extruded white knob riding in it. The knob is a wide capsule rather than a circle, which gives the stretch somewhere to go and makes the elongation legible at a glance. A visible hand cursor performs every action, turning the clip into a self-narrating interaction script; the segmented control doubles as an A/B rig so the viewer feels the delta between "Plain" and "Stretch" rather than being told about it.

## Why saved
No owner note; tweet text: "Switches feel better when they stretch on press" - which maps 1:1 onto the taste profile line "Switches that stretch on press."

What earns it, verified frame-by-frame:
1. **A press state exists at all.** Generic toggles animate only between the two settled states; here mousedown is its own designed pose, holdable indefinitely (~600ms shown), making the gesture interruptible and cancelable.
2. **The stretch is directional information, not decoration.** The knob grows toward its destination while anchored at its origin edge - it scales from the correct transform-origin, so it reads as elastic material reaching for where it will land, previewing the result before commit.
3. **Two synchronized clocks with a deliberate offset.** Track color and knob position animate together, but on release the knob lands ~80–100ms before the color finishes - position leads, color confirms.
4. **Spring energy dissipates as contraction, not wobble.** The elongated knob absorbs its momentum by narrowing back to rest width on arrival; there is no cartoonish bounce.
5. **The demo format is itself craft.** A segmented "Plain / Stretch" A/B rig (whose own thumb slides with the same motion language) lets the viewer feel the difference; the oversized 800px track makes a sub-400ms micro-interaction legible; and the clip loops seamlessly back to frame 1.

## Extractable rules
- Give toggles a distinct mousedown state: stretch the knob 15–25% toward its travel direction, anchored at the origin edge (transform-origin on the trailing side).
- Make the press pose stable and holdable; commit the state change only on release, so the gesture is cancelable.
- Use a wide capsule knob (~60% of track width) instead of a circle when you want deformation to be legible.
- Animate track color and knob position on the same clock (~200–300ms); let position finish ~80–100ms before color on fast toggles.
- Interpolate track color through the actual crossfade (gray #C7C7C7 → slate #AEBBCB → blue #2E6FE9); never hard-swap fills.
- Dissipate spring energy as width contraction on arrival (high damping, ~stiffness 300/damping 30); avoid visible bounce.
- Keep the plain fallback at 180–250ms ease-out with zero deformation - reserve elasticity for pointer-driven moments.
- Build depth with layered soft shadows (track ~0 18px 36px rgba(0,0,0,0.10); knob ~0 8px 16px rgba(0,0,0,0.16)) plus a hairline rim, not borders.
- Add ~5% vertical squish during horizontal stretch to conserve perceived volume.
- When demoing a micro-interaction, ship an A/B toggle (plain vs enhanced) inside the demo itself and reuse the same motion language on the demo chrome.
- Record component demos oversized (element ≥ 50% of canvas width) on a near-white #FBFBFB field and close the loop frame-perfectly.
